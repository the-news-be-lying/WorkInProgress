# 🧬 Involuntary Sterilization in the United States
### A Complete Overview — October 2026

---

## ⚡ The Big Picture

Between **1907 and the 1970s**, the U.S. government forcibly sterilized an estimated **60,000–70,000 people** across **32 states**. These were not criminals or volunteers — they were poor people, people with disabilities, racial minorities, and prisoners who were told (or never told) they were being made permanently infertile under the banner of **"eugenics"** — the discredited pseudoscience that complex traits like poverty or intelligence were "hereditary" and could be bred out of the population.

The practice was **legally sanctioned** by the Supreme Court in *Buck v. Bell* (1927) and was so influential that **Nazi Germany explicitly modeled its 1933 sterilization law on American statutes**.

> 🚨 **This is not over.** As of 2025, **31 states + D.C.** still have laws allowing court-ordered forced sterilization of disabled people. The most recent such laws were passed in **2019** (Iowa and Nevada). Only **2 states** (Alaska and North Carolina) fully ban nonconsensual sterilization of disabled people.

## 👤 Notable Individual Cases

- **Carrie Buck** (1906–1983) — A poor white woman in Virginia, institutionalized at 18 after being raped and giving birth. Labeled "feebleminded" because she was a single mother. Her case became *Buck v. Bell*. She was sterilized in October 1927, months after the ruling. She later said: *"If there's any justice, I ain't suffered so much for my crime as the state has suffered for mine."*

- **Minnie Lee & Mary Alice Relf** — Black sisters from Alabama, ages **12 and 14**, sterilized in 1973. Their illiterate mother signed an "X" on a form she thought authorized birth control shots. The case *Relf v. Weinberger* (1974) exposed that **100,000–150,000 poor people** were being sterilized *per year* under federally funded programs.

- **Fannie Lou Hamer** — The civil rights leader was subjected to a forced hysterectomy in 1965 in Mississippi, reportedly as retaliation for her activism with SNCC.

- **Elaine Riddick** — A Black woman from North Carolina, sterilized at age **14** in 1968 while delivering her first child after being raped at 13.

- **Geynna Buffington** — An incarcerated woman in California whose endometrial ablation (performed without informed consent) was denied compensation **four times** by the state board. After she sued, a judge ruled the board "abused its discretion." She finally received her $35,000 in **October 2024**.

- **Native American women** — A 1976 GAO report found **3,406** sterilized by the Indian Health Service in just four states (1973–1976). Some estimates suggest **40% of Native American women** of childbearing age were sterilized in the 1970s.

## 🏛️ The Machinery Behind It

### The Eugenics Record Office (ERO)
The **ERO**, founded in **1910** at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory by Charles Benedict Davenport, served as the intellectual and lobbying hub of American eugenics until its closure in **1939**. Funded by the **Carnegie Institution** and philanthropists including John Harvey Kellogg, the ERO:

- Amassed **hundreds of thousands of family pedigrees** on Americans deemed "socially inadequate"
- **Sponsored training courses** for aspiring eugenics caseworkers
- **Dispatched field workers** to institutions, prisons, and communities to collect data
- **Lobbied state legislatures** directly, providing model legislation
- Created **registries** of specific groups to identify sterilization candidates

Under Harry H. Laughlin, who became superintendent in 1910, the ERO functioned as much a **lobbying agency** as a research institution. His 1922 book *Eugenical Sterilization in the United States* became the **model text** for state sterilization legislation.

### Other Key Organizations
- **American Eugenics Society (AES)** — Public-facing advocacy; hosted the International Eugenics Congresses (1912, 1921, 1932)
- **Human Betterment Foundation** (1928–1942, Pasadena, CA) — More radical offshoot; explicitly advocated sterilization of "the unfit"
- **The Medical Profession** — The AMA endorsed eugenics; eugenics was taught in university biology departments (e.g., San Francisco State, 1916–1951)
- **Immigration** — The 1924 Johnson-Reed Act was a direct product of eugenic lobbying. *The Passing of the Great Race* by Madison Grant (1916) was a foundational text; Hitler referred to it as his "Bible"

## 🏥 The Medical Procedures

- **Tubal ligation** (for women): Fallopian tubes cut, tied, or removed. In the eugenics era, often performed as an **open abdominal surgery**. At Los Angeles County/USC Medical Center, tubal ligations were sometimes performed **during cesarean sections** without the woman's knowledge.
- **Vasectomy** (for men): Vas deferens severed and sealed. The earliest U.S. eugenic vasectomies were performed by Dr. C. C. Sharp in Oregon beginning in **1899**. By 1909, he had sterilized **280 inmates**.
- **Hysterectomy**: In some cases, particularly in the Medicaid era, the entire uterus was removed.
- **Endometrial ablation**: A procedure that destroys the uterine lining. The California compensation board initially rejected these as "not sterilization" — a position a court ruled was legally wrong in the **Buffington case** (2024).

## 🔁 How Consent Was "Obtained" (or Not)

There was rarely any real consent. Common tactics:

| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| 🎭 **Deception** | Told it was an appendectomy or C-section; sterilized while under anesthesia |
| 🏛️ **Institutional coercion** | "Sign this or you don't get released" |
| ✍️ **Illiteracy / language barriers** | Signed an "X" not knowing what it meant (Relf sisters) |
| 👶 **Age** | Victims as young as 10–14 (Utah's modal age was 15–19) |
| 👨‍👩‍👧 **Third-party consent** | A husband, parent, or institution superintendent signed instead |
| 🌐 **Google Translate** | Used at ICE detention centers instead of proper interpretation |

### The "Feebleminded" Diagnosis as a Tool of Social Control
The category of **"feeblemindedness"** was the most common justification, but it was deeply elastic:
- It encompassed not only people with intellectual disabilities but also the **poor, "promiscuous," or simply non-compliant** — particularly women
- Carrie Buck was classified as "feebleminded" partly because she was a **single mother** who had been raped
- In North Carolina, the eugenics board used it as a catch-all for **Black women** whose poverty was deemed hereditary
- The diagnosis was often applied **retroactively** to justify institutionalization, which then made the person eligible for sterilization

## 🔄 The "Morphing" Pattern: How the Practice Adapts

The history reveals a consistent pattern of the practice **shifting form** while maintaining the same logic:

| Era | Mechanism | Target Population | Legal Cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1907–1940s** | State eugenics boards | "Feebleminded," institutionalized | State sterilization statutes |
| **1940s–1960s** | Decline (Nazi association) | — | — |
| **1960s–1979** | Federal Medicaid family planning | Poor women of color | Federal funding + state laws |
| **1979–2014** | Prison medical programs | Incarcerated people | Inadequate consent standards |
| **2000s–present** | Court-ordered (guardianship) | Disabled people (esp. children) | 31 state laws + *Buck v. Bell* |
| **2010s–present** | Immigration detention | Detained immigrants | ICE medical protocols |

Each phase used a **different legal mechanism** but the same underlying logic: that certain groups' reproductive capacity is a matter for the state to manage.

## 🏛️ The Legal Mechanism Today (2025–2026)

Under the **31 states'** current laws, the process works like this:

1. A **guardian** (family member, state agency, or institutional administrator) petitions a court to authorize sterilization of a person with a disability
2. The court holds a hearing and determines whether the person is "unable to make the decision"
3. The judge decides whether sterilization is in the person's "best interest"
4. If approved, the procedure is performed

**The critical problem**: The legal framework **presumes incapacity** based on disability status. As Lydia X. Z. Brown of the Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network stated:

> *"These laws assume that disabled people can't make decisions about their own bodies—which is not just untrue, it's dangerous."*

About **half** of the 31 states require the person to be under guardianship; the other half allow forced sterilization **with or without** guardianship. **17 states** allow forced sterilization of disabled *children*.

> 🚨 There is **no official count** of how many disabled people have been sterilized under these still-active laws.

## 🏥 The ICE Detention Center Allegations

One of the most alarming modern cases is the **Irwin County Detention Center (ICDC)** in Ocilla, Georgia, housing up to 1,200 immigrants detained by ICE:

- A whistleblower nurse, **Dawn Wooten**, was **demoted** after asking "hard questions" about medical protocols
- Her complaint flagged the **rate of hysterectomies** performed on detained immigrant women, alleging they were done without proper informed consent due to **language barriers** (staff used Google Translate instead of proper Spanish interpretation)
- The ACLU drew explicit parallels to the 1975 *Madrigal v. Quilligan* case, noting that "history tragically repeats itself"
- A 2025 public health journal article (*"It Never Stopped"*) documented a pattern consistent with "a long history of forced, coerced sterilization, intrusive gynecological practices, and 'medically necessary' procedures"

### California's FISCP: Final Numbers (as of Feb. 2025)

California's **Forced or Involuntary Sterilization Compensation Program** (FISCP), administered by the California Victim Compensation Board:

| Metric | Number |
|---|---|
| Total applications received | **574** |
| Approved | **133** |
| Denied | **417** |
| Approval rate | **~23%** |
| Total compensation paid | **$4,655,000** |
| Per-victim payment | **$35,000** |
| Denied applications from currently incarcerated male inmates | **205** |

Key problems:
- 📉 **~77% of applicants were denied**
- 🚫 The board repeatedly rejected **endometrial ablations** as "not sterilization" — a position a court ruled was legally wrong in the **Buffington case** (2024)
- 👴 **Only 3 survivors** from the original 1909–1979 eugenics era applied and were approved
- 📅 The program **stopped accepting new applications on December 31, 2023**. Appeals were extended to **January 1, 2025**. The program **officially ends in January 2026**
- 🔥 A **data breach** in 2023 exposed the personal and medical information of applicants
- 📊 Among applicants who volunteered demographic info, the **majority self-identified as Black or African American**
- 📊 Approximately **47% of applicants were male, 40% female, 4% transgender**

> *"They were hellbent on denying this claim, I believe, because they know they have failed an entire class of people."* — Cynthia Chandler, attorney who helped draft the law

The **IRS** (2023) confirmed that state compensation payments are **excluded from gross income** — survivors do not owe taxes on reparations.

### Why So Few Survivors Applied

- ⏰ **Age and mortality**: Most victims were sterilized in the 1920s–1950s; many have died
- 🤫 **Stigma and silence**: Survivors were often told what happened was "for their own good"
- 📋 **Documentation barriers**: Many survivors lacked medical records
- 🏚️ **Incarceration**: 205 denied applicants were *currently incarcerated*
- 📉 **Distrust**: After being denied by the system once, many survivors did not trust a second system
- 📢 **Lack of outreach**: Advocates criticized the state for inadequate outreach

## 🧠 Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

1. 🇺🇸 This was **state-sponsored**, legally sanctioned, and medically performed — not a fringe operation
2. 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 It targeted the **poor, disabled, and people of color** — overwhelmingly women
3. ⚖️ The Supreme Court **blessed it** in 1927 and never formally overturned the ruling
4. 🇩🇪 The **Nazis copied** American eugenics law — this wasn't a foreign aberration; it originated here
5. 🔄 The practice **morphed** from state eugenics boards → federal Medicaid → prisons → detention centers → court-ordered sterilization of disabled people, and echoes persist today
6. 💰 Compensation has been **limited, late, and partial** — most victims never received acknowledgment
7. 🚨 **It's still legal in 31 states** for court-ordered sterilization of disabled people, and the practice continues in prisons and detention centers under different names
